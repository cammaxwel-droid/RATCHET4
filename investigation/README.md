# Ratchet-4 V1: NS3 warning investigation

V1 remains an experimental, runnable prototype. This investigation found strong evidence that the most alarming PractRand NS3 warnings involve poor small-sample calibration. It did not find a new, established defect in the V1 recurrence, and it does not establish the generator's quality, security, fairness, or production suitability.

The full **unmodified PractRand 0.96 executable reported `FAIL` on an external AES control stream** in the same subtest that warned about V1. Source tracing and an independent calculation explain why a normal-tail interpretation is misleading here: the score was computed from a single pair of selected blocks.

The approved V1 engine, its seeds, constants, bounded mapping, demo, original evidence, and delivered ZIP were not changed. No replacement design was implemented. AES appears only as external diagnostic input generated by OpenSSL; it is not part of the candidate engine or game.

## Executed findings

The focused adapter linked the original PractRand 0.96 library and used its actual low-bit transforms and NS3 implementation. It examined three projections, all returned NS3 size indices, and checkpoints of 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64 MiB per stream.

| Input | Checkpoint | NS3 subtest | Raw score | Reported approximate p |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| V1 `(2^63, 0)` | 32 MiB | `[Low1/16]NS3[5:pd:both]` | 5.998213026 | 9.975035e-10 |
| V1 `(2^63, 0)` | 32 MiB | `[Low4/32]NS3[6:hw:both]` | 5.693126740 | 6.236686e-9 |
| AES control 11 | 16 MiB | `[Low1/16]NS3[5:pd:both]` | 7.045350029 | 9.249793e-13 |

The sparse-seed pair-distance score is exactly unchanged at 64 MiB: no new qualifying core was added. Those two checkpoints are correlated observations of the same pair, not two independent failures.

The fixed panel contained 32 AES-256-CTR control streams with public keys 0 through 31, 16 V1 raw streams with explicitly listed boundary/nearby/patterned seeds, and the reversed-bit V1 zero-seed stream. Every stream supplied 64 MiB: **49 cases, 3,288,334,336 bytes (3.0625 GiB)**. Replayed V1 prefixes and reversed projections overlap earlier testing; this is not a claim of independent new samples.

For the three target subtests chosen from the original warnings, an unadjusted approximate p below 1e-8 occurred in 1/32 AES streams, 1/16 V1 raw streams, and the separately reported reversed zero-seed stream. These are descriptive counts, not a formal equivalence test or a corrected family-wise significance level. Checkpoints, projections, and pair statistics are correlated. The panel is too small to estimate rare-event rates reliably.

The full-suite command on control 11 requested a 64 MiB maximum, but **stopped at the 16 MiB checkpoint after reporting `FAIL`**, with exit status 0. Its log reports 35.9 seconds at that checkpoint. An exit status of zero is therefore not evidence of a statistical pass. The focused adapter completed all checkpoints through 64 MiB for all 49 cases.

## What the small-sample calculation establishes

Count-only tracing was added to a separate diagnostic build of PractRand; no selection, scoring, or candidate code was changed. Original and traced CSV results matched byte for byte for the sparse seed, control 11, and reversed zero seed.

For the sparse-seed pair-distance warning, NS3 selected **two 2048-bit cores**, producing **one pair**. Their two concatenated neighboring regions contain 4096 bits and differ at 1942 positions. Control 11 also had two cores and one pair; its distance was 2163. An independent NumPy implementation reproduced the selected core locations and measured these distances directly from the original input files.

Under an ideal model of independent, uniform neighboring bits, the distance H has a Binomial(4096, 1/2) distribution. Its exact discrete two-sided tail is:

`min(1, 2 * sum(comb(4096, j), j=0..min(H,4096-H)) / 2^4096)`.

| Input | Distance | Ideal independent-neighborhood tail | NS3 approximate p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| V1 sparse seed | 1942 | 0.0009753660781 | 9.975035e-10 |
| AES control 11 | 2163 | 0.0003449310121 | 9.249793e-13 |

These reference tails are **not corrected p-values for NS3's entire adaptive selection procedure**, nor are they proof that these V1 bits are independent. They demonstrate the scale of the discrepancy for the one-pair statistic under the explicit ideal model. The reference-tail calculation passed 90 exhaustive comparisons with direct enumeration at bit lengths 1 through 12.

NS3 transforms a folded Hamming-weight cumulative probability with `-log(CDF)`, centers/scales it, then applies a normal-tail conversion. Centering and scaling a single skewed observation does not make it normally distributed. The discrepancy above, the observed sample counts, and the independent control failure support a calibration explanation for the strongest warning. The sparse-seed Hamming-weight warning used only three selected cores. The reversed-zero warning used three cores and three pair comparisons; its full joint null distribution was not derived here. Other earlier warnings have not all been individually resolved.

## Interpretation and limits

**Established by execution:** the original warnings reproduce; the external control also triggers the original suite's failure label; the strongest pair-distance examples use one pair; tracing preserves scores; the approved engine and original package hashes are unchanged.

**Established under a stated mathematical model:** the tabulated binomial tails for independent uniform neighboring regions; standardization alone does not imply a normal distribution.

**Supported inference:** small-sample NS3 tail calibration substantially overstates the apparent rarity of these particular observations. This is evidence against treating the printed approximate probabilities as literal calibrated significance levels.

**Not established:** that every warning is a false alarm; that V1 is unbiased or unpredictable; its exact period; seed-path uniformity; originality against all prior literature; suitability for real-money use. Passing other diagnostics cannot establish these properties. No seed filtering, output correction, or change of constants was used.

Retain V1 as the approved research prototype. These findings do not justify a replacement merely to suppress the NS3 labels. Stronger quality claims would require broader testing with independently calibrated tests and further mathematical analysis. Any replacement engine still requires a separate proposal and approval.

## Evidence and reproduction

- `evidence/control-panel/plan.json`: fixed case list and tool/binary identifiers, written before the panel ran.
- `evidence/control-panel/manifest.json`: exact commands, input SHA-256 hashes, sizes, and per-case wall times.
- `evidence/control-panel/*.csv`: every returned result, including ordinary results and repeated checkpoints.
- `evidence/aes-control-11-full-suite.txt`: original suite's control failure; stderr is also retained.
- `evidence/trace-*.txt`, `trace-*.csv`: count traces and identical-score checks.
- `evidence/analysis.json`: panel counts, all per-stream worst target results, and independent core/pair measurements.
- `evidence/verification.json`: verification results and the corrected analysis-harness assumption about the reversed case's core count.
- `evidence/*build.json`: compiler commands and diagnostic-source hashes.
- `REPRODUCING.md`: commands, dependencies, versions, and source provenance.

The scripts, adapter, and documentation are available under 0BSD. PractRand's separate CC0/public-domain notice is retained in `third-party/PractRand-license.txt`. OpenSSL is an external test dependency and is not redistributed here. No GitHub service was accessed.

## Primary references

PractRand explicitly marks crude probabilities with `p~=` and distinguishes their quality from calibrated results. Its `TestResult::TYPE_BAD_S` comment calls that category extremely crude. The exact NS3 implementation is in the original 0.96 archive, `src/tests_other.cpp` and `include/PractRand/Tests/NearSeq.h`. See the [official interpretation guide](https://pracrand.sourceforge.net/Tests_overview.txt) and [original 0.96 release](https://sourceforge.net/projects/pracrand/files/PractRand_0.96.zip/download).
